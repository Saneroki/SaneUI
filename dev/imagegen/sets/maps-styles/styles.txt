# Five art styles for the painted map view (Atlas), each on four subjects, to choose from: no slight variations but

# grimy realism that stays true to the old World of Warcraft, and high fantasy, to compare.
#   grimy    gritty, weathered realism, like an old aerial photograph
#   classic  the original game's hand-painted textures and concept paintings
#   epic     high-fantasy matte painting, cinematic light
#   oil      a dark old-master oil painting, varnished and grimy
#   render   a photorealistic modern game-engine render
# Image 1 is the subject's terrain (the game's minimap textures, the water flat), the one picture; the style is in
# words. The words name no place: the model writes legible names into the picture.

grimy-stormwind: Repaint image 1 as a gritty, weathered realistic view from a great height, like an old aerial photograph on an overcast day: worn and dirty stone, soot-stained roofs, muddy rutted roads, moss and lichen, dense natural woods, muted earthy colours with deep shadows and a light haze; old, rough and lived-in.
classic-stormwind: Repaint image 1 in the hand-painted art style of the original World of Warcraft from 2004: painterly ground textures with visible brush strokes, slightly exaggerated sturdy shapes, a warm earthy palette, soft painted light, the look of the game's own painted textures and early concept paintings, not cartoonish.
epic-stormwind: Repaint image 1 as an epic high-fantasy matte painting seen from above, like Warcraft's cinematic concept art: rich, deep, natural colours, dramatic golden sunlight breaking through drifting clouds, glowing atmosphere and light rays, crisp architectural detail, majestic and heroic.

render-stormwind: Repaint image 1 as a photorealistic modern game-engine render seen from above: physically based stone, wood, roof tiles and foliage, crisp sunlight with soft shadows and ambient occlusion, clear water with reflections and shallows, extremely detailed, like a high-end remaster.
