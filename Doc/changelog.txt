Sane UI: what is new
====================

The bigger changes of each version, newest first.


0.3.0 (2026-10-14)
------------------
- The chat window's full screen holds more than the chat: the friends
  list at the left and the group finder at the right, both as the
  game's own, and a second chat above the chat that shows only the
  chats you tick for it (whispers, groups and guild at first). Each can
  be switched off (Options > Social > General > Full screen). The
  search in the title bar finds in all of them.

- Profiles, made simple: with "Accountwide settings" on (as at first),
  all your characters share one profile, Accountwide: Sane UI's
  settings and the game's own (its options, Edit Mode layout, chat
  windows). Make more profiles and give one to any character, copy one
  from another, reset one to its defaults, or export it as text to share
  and import it again. With Accountwide settings off, each character
  keeps its own Sane UI settings and the game's settings are left alone.
  /sane profile lists your profiles and switches to one.

- Sane UI's windows: Esc closes them as it closes the game's panels, a
  click brings a panel to the front, and they have tabs like the game's.
  In Edit Mode a click on a window selects it, with "Reset To Default
  Position", and windows snap to Edit Mode's grid. "Reset window
  positions" (Options > General, or /sane window reset all) puts every
  window back. Key bindings: a section "Sane UI" with keys for the
  options and the chat window. Animations can be switched off for all
  of Sane UI (Options > Appearance).

- The chat window starts docked in the lower left corner and has no bar
  of its own.

- Sane UI's options: settings you type in, dropdowns where you tick
  several, and buttons. A page with tabs (Social) has a search box that
  finds a setting in any tab, and a Defaults button. /sane options
  during a fight opens the options when the fight is over. Tooltips'
  settings come in sections.

- The game settings Sane UI keeps no longer stall the game when a
  character comes into the world, and settings the game does not let
  addons change are left alone. An Edit Mode layout only one character
  has stays that character's choice when another character logs out.

- Abilities: saving, loading and deleting an action bar layout asks in
  the game's own dialogs. A switch flipped in the spellbook's menu shows
  on the options page at once.

- Tooltips have a cleaner look in the game's own colours. A spell's
  tooltip shows only what the game says about it, now in every language.
  Quest tooltips offer Skyborne characters the quests meant for their
  race, show rare drops as "<1%", and creatures say "Next level in N".

- Abilities: hunters no longer see their pet's abilities as grey spells,
  and a spell that needs another spell first says which one.

- The game settings Sane UI keeps the same on all characters now follow
  the game's own options pages. The game's chat channels are chosen in
  the chat window's channel list.

- Windows that open under the mouse wait a moment before they open and
  close, and can be kept open. The CVar list lets you edit values in
  place and filter them with the game's filter button.

- The Sane UI page in the game's options (Esc > Options > AddOns >
  Sane UI) shows where to find Sane UI (Discord, website, GitHub) and
  has buttons to report a bug, suggest a feature or send feedback. Write
  it there, and Sane UI makes a report to copy and paste on GitHub or
  Discord. Also /sane feedback.

- Windows get a title bar with their name and their buttons (the game's
  own window, maximize and minimize, close). Drag it to move the window.
  Click the name to search the window: type a few letters, in order, and
  only what matches stays (a full screen chat finds messages, speakers and
  chats that way). A window remembers whether you left it maximized.

- Tooltips: a creature's or player's portrait is now its live 3D model,
  moving, its face in view. Where the game cannot draw it, the picture
  stays as before. The setting Live 3D portraits switches it off.

- The Spellbook module is now called Abilities: it will do more than the
  spellbook. Its settings and reservations carry over. Type /sane abilities
  (or /sane ab); the old /sane spellbook still works.

- Windows can dock in the middle of the screen too, the place for windows
  you talk to (vendor, trainer, auction house; to come). A window in the
  middle or not docked has buttons in its top right corner: the game's own
  window instead (and on the game's window a button to come back),
  maximize and minimize, and close. In Social the first and the last bring
  the game's chat back.

- Sane UI's windows are moved and sized like the game's own frames: only in
  Edit Mode (Esc > Edit Mode). There each shows Edit Mode's blue box with
  its name; drag it, and size it by its grip. Outside Edit Mode there is no
  grip and nothing is dragged by accident.

- Social: a smiley at the end of the line you write in. It puts the game's
  raid marks into your message (where the game's chat shows them: not in
  General, Trade and its other public channels) and does the emotes played
  most, such as /hello, /cheer and /thank.

- Chat is now called Social: it is to hold everything social, the chat
  first. Your settings come along; /sane chat still works next to
  /sane social.

- A window that fills the screen has a dropdown Classic windows in its top
  right corner with the game's own windows it stands for. In Social: Chat
  puts our chat window away and brings the game's chat back (/sane social
  brings ours again); Friends list opens the game's friends list. A window
  that fills the screen is always square; only a small window is round.

- Windows get round corners and a thin bronze trim, square where they meet
  the edge of the screen. The chat window shows them as it grows to fill
  the screen. Under Social > Appearance > Expand window (first in the tab
  again): Corner radius, and Don't round corners on screen edges (off:
  every corner is round; at 100 % a square window becomes a circle).

- /sane window: a window to try Sane UI's windows; click it to keep it
  open.

- Tooltips: what you wear stands still beside the item you look at, a small
  gap between them, instead of flickering between two places.

- Tooltips: the Gold section says what one item brings at a vendor and at
  the auction house after the cut, by the prices you saw there yourself
  (every item shows its vendor price, also in a stack: for one), and tells
  you in one line what to do: sell it at the auction house for a price, sell
  it to a vendor, or at the auction house the most it is worth buying for.
  Hold Alt for the numbers behind it.

- Tooltips: an item's quests in a Quest section of their own: the quest it
  starts and every quest that needs it, each saying whether it is in your
  quest log (with your count), available to take, not yet for you, or done.
  Creatures show items they drop that start a quest; every quest name has
  its level. A class or profession an
  item asks for stands at the right under the level it needs.

- Spellbook: move a reservation like any button. Drag its grey icon on the
  action bar to another button, with the bars unlocked or Shift held. Let
  go where there is no button and the reservation is removed.

- Spellbook: drag a grey spell onto an empty button even where the bar hides
  its empty buttons ("always show buttons" off). The
  reservation shows on that spot.

- Spellbook: in every tab the spells you know come first, then the ones you
  can learn now, then the rest in the order you will get them.

- Every setting explains itself: hover a checkbox, slider or dropdown
  and its tooltip says in a sentence or two what it does and what
  "Off" means. Labels say what they are: Chat's Fade in, Fade out and
  Slide up with their times, Bubble opacity (100 solid, like every
  other opacity slider), Show the game's chat too; the Spellbook's
  Show unlearned spells, Move and resize the spellbook.

- Spellbook: its options page has two sections. "Sane UI lays out the
  spellbook pages" comes first; the four switches that need it are
  greyed out while it is off. The spellbook's own menu has the switches
  about the book too. Deleting a saved layout asks first. Fixed: a
  layout saved in a fight lost the buttons the game hid.

- Chat: a switch "Chat window" shows or hides the window; the ticks in
  the list on the chat's name follow your profiles now.

- Esc brings a window that fills the screen back.

- Many fixes after a full review: a character whose profile was
  Empty lost its changes at every logout; windows docked to a screen
  edge stay there when you change the UI scale; the spellbook keeps
  its height when you make it small or large; item and unit levels
  show when tooltip icons are off; the chat keeps your place while
  you read back, and shows raid marks as icons.

- Profiles: Empty, first in the list and what every character has at
  first: your settings as they are kept, loaded at every login and saved
  at every logout and /reload. SaneUI Defaults now only resets Sane UI's
  own settings, not the game settings that Sync keeps.

- Sync: the Edit Mode layout you choose is kept, also in your profiles.
  It was set back to Modern at every login.

- Chat: saves your chat history (the last 100 messages at first, up to
  500) and shows it again at the next login. System, creature and
  addon messages, and channel joins and leaves, each have a switch
  in Chat > General; joins and leaves are hidden at first. The
  bubbles stand closer together.

- Chat: all bubbles look alike, yours too. Choose their background in
  Chat > Appearance: a solid color of your choice, or one of five of
  the game's own background textures (Marble, Rock, Slate, Iron, Night).

- Chat: everything the game's chat shows is in the chat window too:
  system messages, pings, loot, money, experience, battleground and
  channel notices, friends coming online, addon messages. Each kind
  can be shown or hidden in the list on the chat's name.

- Settings and Sync are part of Sane UI itself now, no addons of their
  own. If you installed an earlier 0.3.0, delete the folders
  SaneUI_Settings and SaneUI_Sync from Interface\AddOns.

- Profiles: the profile SaneUI Defaults is always there, with every
  setting as Sane UI has it at first. Load it to go back to those
  settings, or duplicate it to start a profile of your own. It cannot be
  changed, renamed or deleted.

- Profiles: the line New Characters in the list of characters sets the
  profile, Load at login and Save at logout that a character starts
  with when it first logs in: a new one, or one you have not played
  since installing Sane UI.

- Windows docked to a screen edge fill the whole screen when you push
  the mouse against that edge (docked in a corner: all the way into
  that corner), and spring back when you push it there again. While a
  window fills the screen, nothing behind it can be clicked. How they
  move is chosen under Sane UI > Appearance (Animation type). The chat
  window does it; its section Expand window (Chat >
  Appearance) switches it on or off and sets its own animation type and
  the background's opacity and texture.

- New: Sync, part of Sane UI itself: the settings the game keeps for each character on its
  own are the same on all your characters and part of your profiles, in
  parts you tick, from the Edit Mode layout and the chat windows to
  graphics and sound.

- Chat: clicks on the chat window go through to the game, except on
  links, names and the chat button. Click a name to whisper to them.
  The scroll bar is only there while you are scrolled up. System
  messages, emotes and achievements are bubbles too, in the middle; the
  guild's message of the day and guild achievements are shown. Faded
  messages come back with the mouse wheel, not the mouse alone. New
  messages fade in and the others slide up smoothly. The options page
  Chat has two tabs, General and Appearance; Appearance sets the text,
  the fade in and out speeds, how long messages are shown, how visible
  faded ones stay, the slide speed, or switches all animations off.

- Chat: the game's chat channels (General, Trade, ...) can be joined or
  left on every character at once, on the page Chat of the options.

- Options: a list of every CVar of the game. Search it, see what each one
  does and its default, change it, and tick it to keep it the same on all
  your characters.

- Options: a clearer layout. Sane UI shows its logo; under it are the pages
  Profiles, General and Appearance, then one page per module, called by its
  name (Chat, Spellbook, Tooltips).

- Profiles: a page of their own, with tabs: Profiles, Accountwide and
  CVars. All settings are account wide at first; untick "All
  settings accountwide" and every character keeps its own, or untick single
  modules in the tab Accountwide. Save the settings of every module as a
  profile, and load, duplicate, rename or delete profiles. In the list of
  your characters, give any character a profile to load at login and save
  into at logout: it uses that profile instead of the account's settings.

- Options: every page of Sane UI now has its banner at the top. The
  general page shows the Sane UI banner above General, and each module's
  page shows its own (Sane Spellbook, Sane Chat, Sane Tooltips).

- Windows dock to the screen's edges. Drag the chat window near a corner, the
  middle of an edge, or the middle of the screen, and it snaps there and stays
  there. Drag it away to free it. Type
  /sane window for a small window to try it with: that one also springs open
  when you move the mouse onto it.

- Spellbook: a Home page. The spellbook opens on a new first tab that shows
  what your trainer can teach you right now, with prices, and which spells you
  get from quests and where.

- Spellbook: marks on the icons. A yellow "!" means you get the spell from a
  quest, not from the trainer. A small
  clock means a button is reserved for it; the reserved button on your action
  bar carries the same clock and shows the spell's tooltip.

- Spellbook: the old training tab and window are gone. Home replaces them.

- Spellbook: move it and resize it. Drag the bar at the top to move the
  spellbook, drag its lower right corner to give it any size. The pages fill
  the window, and they are more compact: about twice as many spells on a page.

- Spellbook: all ranks of a spell fold into one entry. Click the + on it to see
  every rank, and the heading above them to fold them away again.

- Spellbook: the General tab lists the passive spells and the weapon skills you
  can still learn. A checkbox next to the page buttons hides and shows all the
  grey entries.

- Modules are called by their plain name in the addon list: Spellbook,
  Tooltips, Chat. The options' controls are built when you first open them.

- New module: Chat, a first try. A chat window that looks like a messenger:
  every message in a bubble, yours on the right, everybody else's on the left
  with name and class icon. Drag its top bar to move it and its lower right
  corner to size it. It takes the place of the game's chat: press Enter or /
  and the line to write in opens at its bottom. Click the chat's name in that
  line (Say, Guild) to choose where you write. Messages fade out after a
  while; move the mouse over the window to read them again. You can switch
  the fading off or set its seconds in the options. The same list lets you
  tick which chats the window shows. Point at a linked item, spell or quest
  to see its tooltip; click it as in the game's chat. Tick Chat in the addon
  list; /sane chat switches between this window and the game's own chat. The game's
  chat stays on screen next to it unless you switch "Show native chat" off.

- New module: Tooltips. The game's tooltips are drawn anew on dark panels in
  the colour of what they are about. Items show their damage, level, durability
  and price at a glance; spells their cost, cast time, cooldown and range side
  by side; players and creatures a portrait, health and mana bars, their target
  and their buffs; buffs how long they still run; quests what is left to do.
  Spells that hurt or heal also show their damage, damage per second, damage
  per mana and your crit chance; creatures the experience a kill gives and how
  many kills to your next level, and what your quests still want from them (to
  kill or to loot, with how often they drop it); quest givers the quests they offer you and take back; items
  their vendor price and the auction price you last saw and, for trade goods, the
  professions that use them.
  In the options you can have tooltips show only while you hold Shift, Ctrl,
  Alt, or Shift and Alt, and have them follow the mouse cursor always or while
  you hold a key. A tooltip is gone as soon as the mouse leaves what it is
  about, without fading out slowly. A link clicked in chat opens its tooltip
  in the middle of the screen; click the tooltip to close it. You can also set the text size and the opacity. Tick
  Tooltips in the addon list; untick it and
  the tooltips are the game's own again.

- Fixed: Sane UI's options were missing from Esc > Options > AddOns when no
  other addon had options of its own. They are back.

- Chat: in a fight or a dungeon, where the game shows addons no chat, the
  game's own chat comes back until it is over, so no message is missed.

- Lighter and sturdier everywhere: the spellbook does its heavy work only when
  you open it, the chat does less per message, and a part that fails gives the
  game's own back without taking the rest of Sane UI with it.


0.2.0 (2026-10-14)
------------------
- Modules you can switch on and off. In the game's addon list, Sane UI now has
  its modules under it: Settings and Spellbook. Untick the ones
  you do not want. What a module saved is kept until you switch it on again.

- Options with a page per module. Esc > Options > AddOns > Sane UI is the
  general page; each module that is switched on has its own page under it.

- If you update from 0.1.0: copy all three folders (SaneUI, SaneUI_Settings,
  SaneUI_Spellbook) into your AddOns folder and restart the game.


0.1.0 (2026-10-14)
------------------
The first version of Sane UI.

- Spells you have not learned yet, in your spellbook. They are listed in grey,
  in the tab where they will end up, with the level or trainer they come from.

- Reserve action bar buttons. Drag a grey spell onto a button: the button waits
  for it and becomes the real spell the moment you learn it.

- Training: a tab in the spellbook shows what your trainer has for you, and
  what it costs.

- Action bar layouts. Save all your bars under a name and load them again later.

- Options in the game's own menu: Esc > Options > AddOns > Sane UI, or type /sane.
