Sane UI: what is new
====================

The bigger changes of each version, newest first.


0.3.0 (2026-10-14)
------------------
- Fixed: WoW: Forever crashed in the Ruins of Lordaeron while Sane UI
  was on: its profiles set water detail to its finest. Sane UI now
  leaves water detail to the game's own graphics menu.
- Threat and damage meter: Lock window, in its cog and on the page
  Combat, keeps it where it is and at its size. It no longer shows on
  or over the full screen.
- Quest timers: a timed quest's time left counts down at the top of the
  quest log, and stays in sight while the log is hidden at rest or
  switched off.
- Atlas: the painted map shows the whole of the Eastern Kingdoms and
  most of Kalimdor, painted from straight above; Kalimdor's south
  follows.
- Routes never use your hearthstone. When it would get you to the
  map pin sooner, the chat tells you once, with the minutes it saves.
- Party frames: right of each member of a party, two small frames half
  their size, what they target and their combat pet, each with its name
  alone. The party and raid frames show each player's health in their
  class's colour.
- Edit auras: one window for every aura's setup, its marks and its icon
  on the health bars (/sane auras, /sane reminders, or the button on the
  Aura icons settings). Search any spell, tick its Show on healthbars,
  and set where its icon shows and on whom right under the list. What was
  called a reminder is now Show on healthbars.
- Aura lists (/sane auras): one list of every aura you set up, a tick
  for each mark and the X in front to take it off, and a search over
  every class's auras, the enemies' and food, potions and items, grouped
  by class as you type. Show on unit frames is now called Prioritize on
  unit frames.
- Straight to the service: a vendor, flight master, trainer or banker
  that has no quest for you opens its window at once, without the talk
  before it. Hold Shift as you talk to them to see the talk and its
  choices (Lore, on by default).
- Aura icons on your health bars: tick an aura's Show on healthbars
  (/sane auras) and its icon shows on your own frame, your target's,
  your focus's and each party or raid member's while they lack it, your
  Mark of the Wild or your Rejuvenation, say. Only yours counts your own
  cast alone. In a fight a HoT that runs out shows at once, and one you
  cast on someone goes from their frame. The buffs of your class no
  longer show there by themselves.
- Someone joining your group no longer breaks Sane UI's party frames,
  bars and unit frames, which then gave way to the game's own.
- Quest areas on the small map are Sane UI's alone and lighter: the
  game's minimap no longer draws its own over them, nor the white ring
  that showed round them.
- Indoor maps now show in Ironforge's halls, the Undercity and the other
  halls and tunnels you may ride through, over the zone's map, instead of
  the mountain or the ruins above them.
- A druid's Action Bar 6 swaps with your form: Bar 9 in Bear Form, Bar 10
  in Cat, Aquatic and Travel Form, Bar 6 itself as a human. Beside it, as
  beside Action Bar 1, the number of the bar it shows, arrows up and down
  to show Bars 6, 9 and 10 without shifting (to fill them, say), and a
  checkbox whether it swaps with your form. A Sane Macro put on Bar 9 or
  10 swaps with it too.
- Sane Macros on your action bars act like any button: drop a spell or an
  item on one to put that there instead, or drag the macro with Shift to
  another button or off the bars.
- The game's message "You have unopened items in your collection", once
  closed, no longer comes back at every login until something new waits.
- Speed Leveling in a party: one guide for all of you. Two of you on the
  same guide now get the same steps by the same numbers, every quest of
  the guide for both, whatever your classes. A class's own steps (its
  trainer, its class quests) are that class's alone: the other sees them
  faint and waits at the next step you share. Whoever is through with a
  step first waits on it (its card says for whom) and keeps killing what
  it asks, until the other is through with it too; then you go on
  together. Switch: Together with the party.
- Speed Leveling no longer adds steps for your professions by itself;
  tick Professions along the way to have them again.
- Sane Macros: click a macro's picture to choose another. A large window
  shows every icon of the game, sorted into groups (each class, weapons,
  armor, consumables, trade goods, spells, mounts, achievements and more)
  with a search box; the mouse on an icon names it and says which spells
  and items use it.
- Sane Macros shows your macros as squares of their pictures with their
  names under them, as the game's macro window does. Dragging one onto
  an action bar keeps the bars clear: its picture follows the mouse and
  the button under it lights up in gold. On Combat's full screen the
  action bars stand under Sane Macros, stacked as on your screen.
- The minimap's satchel has a Sound line: pick a sound profile (All
  sound on, Music off, Music and Ambience off, All sound off, or your
  own), and the
  cogwheel beside it holds the game's sound switches and sliders with
  Save, Load, New and Delete. Each game account remembers its last
  profile and sets it again when you log in, so two accounts played at
  once on one computer can each keep their own sound.
- A warrior whose talents need a shield (Protection, Shield Slam and the
  like) now picks one-handed weapons and shields, never a two-hander, for
  quest rewards, group rolls and the upgrade arrows.
- The map now shows exactly where your quests' objectives are: a pin
  at every place a creature to kill, something to loot or someone to
  speak to can be found, for the quests in your log. Move the mouse
  over a quest in the quest log and its pins glow a little. The pin
  filter has a switch for them.
- Combat's full screen now holds Sane Macros and your action bars as
  windows of their own, so nothing lies over the bars, and you can
  drag a macro straight onto a bar there.
- The game's red error text at the top of the screen is hidden, with a
  switch in Combat's settings to bring it back.
- Sane Macros, a new macro window. A macro is a list of steps, as long
  as you like, with no limit of 30: one step for a normal macro, more
  for a sequence that goes on with each press. Every condition of the
  game's macros is one click away behind a + button, explained. Put a
  macro on a key or on a normal action bar button. It marks what would
  not work. Open it with /macro.
- The satchel on the small map closes when the mouse leaves it, until
  you press its button again. Your times, FPS and ping stand at its
  top in four boxes with big numbers.
- Group rolls look like the loot window now: one bar for the thing,
  with the game's icons for Need, Greed and Pass on it and the time
  left along its foot, running down from green to red, the seconds
  on it. Once you have chosen it fades until the roll is over, then
  it shows for a few seconds who got the thing.
- The threat and damage meter hides out of a fight and when you are
  not in a group, and comes back with the mouse on its place. Right
  click a name to give one character bars of their own.
- Your unit frames and action bars rest out of sight: they show with
  the mouse on them, while your health or resource is not full, in a
  fight and in a group, and the mouse on any action bar shows them
  all. The quest log on the screen does the same. All on by default,
  with a setting each.
- Click a buff or debuff to mark it Important (the game's
  golden animated outline, first on your unit frames), Unimportant
  (faded, never on your unit frames) or Hidden (out of the list until
  you mouse over it). Show on unit frames and Hide from unit frames
  do that part alone, with no outline or fade. A right click on a
  spell in your spellbook opens the same menu, and right clicking a
  buff still cancels it.
- Reminders: click a buff or debuff and choose Create reminder.
  Its icon shows on an invisible bar you place in Edit Mode: when it
  is missing or active, in or out of combat, not in cities if you
  like, and with an offset some seconds before it runs out. Make as
  many bars as you like, one spell or more on each.
- A threat and damage meter: your party or raid, you on top, with a
  bar each for threat, damage, healing and interrupts, no background,
  and clicks go through it. Mouse over it for its page arrows, the
  saved fights and what it shows; drag your own line to move it.
- A pass over everything: bugs fixed in every module, and the parts
  that talk to other players hardened. Among what you will notice: a
  profile's Defaults no longer resets your key bindings; the game's
  cast bar stays on a plate while our cast bar is off, and the game's
  hidden aura icons no longer pop their tooltip beside our bar; the
  bag sort finishes a join that does not fit; a rare item dropped on
  the bin asks the typed confirmation again; the loot window brings
  back a thing the game refused; the auction house's seller, time
  left and repair cost stand under Details in a tooltip; the guide
  key comes back after a fight at login; a pasted guide with no step
  for your character is kept but not chosen, and says so.
- See-through rings around the mouse show your global cooldown and your
  cast while they run. They stand around the whole cursor and start at
  its tip.
- Unit frames show their auras in one line above them: buffs on the
  left, crowd control in the middle, debuffs on the right.
- The game's experience, stance and bag bars and its quest tracker are
  gone from the screen, and its menu bar shows only under the mouse.
- The buttons of your action bars touch: no space left between the
  icons, and bars snapped together in Edit Mode touch as well.
- Action Bar 1 stays on the page you chose: stances, forms and stealth
  no longer change it, only its arrows and your page keys do. Bars 6
  to 8 stand beside its pages, so every page and bar can hold buttons
  of its own.
- Your action bars follow your new ranks: learn a new rank of a spell
  and the buttons with the rank you had until now change to it. A lower
  rank you keep on a button on purpose stays.
- The database follows Forever's release client: items, spells,
  talents and recipes as they are now, walking routes on the ground as
  it now lies. Quests show their experience, money and rewards, and
  how many of each thing they ask for; vendors show what things cost;
  weapons their damage; creatures their rank and the spells they cast.
  Drop rates from the start, rare gems from nodes among them, and the
  map shades where many quests' objectives are found.
- Your professions level up along your guide, whatever your race or
  profession: it tells you to mine or pick herbs where it passes the
  most nodes anyway, checks once more before a harder zone, sends you
  to a herb or ore close to your way right now (and back on track if
  somebody takes it first), and has tailors kill on for cloth while
  the enemies are worth it. Teldrassil's amber nodes are on the map.
- Class quests in your guide, for every class of WoW: Forever: when
  you reach the level of a class quest chain for your class, race and
  side (Bear Form at 10, a warlock's pets, a shaman's totems), the
  guide shows you where to go, step by step, and goes on after it.
- The guide's arrow follows the route to your next goal, by flight
  and boat too, wherever your map pin stands, and says which ride
  comes next. It stands where the level-up and travel overlays
  stand and makes way while they show.
- The advice and the tab Deals weigh your faction's auction house
  against a neutral one, each with its own cut and deposit: buy in
  one and sell in the other where the trip pays.
- A new tab Deals on the auction house window, open first: what to
  buy right now and what of yours to sell, on any character, each
  a click away, and how much gold you have on sale in the corner.
- Money is written 1g1s1c everywhere, with the game's coin icons.
- Tooltips tell when to buy and sell an item for a profit, more and
  more as your auction scans build up: the most worth paying, when it
  fetches most and what one brings then, how many cost less, when it
  is cheapest, what sells. Learns time of day, weekday and trends per
  item, with the house's cut and the post of at least 1s counted.
- One party and raid frame: no matter the group, the same frame with
  more slots, up to forty, in the look of the nameplates, growing up
  and to the right. Set its sizes in Edit Mode, with a preview of
  any number of made-up units. Shows a tank's or healer's mana and,
  over the name, which of your buffs someone is missing.
  Your own slot opens the group's tools (ready check, raid markers
  and more) in place of the raid manager at the edge of the screen.
- Barriers, lost maximum health and dispellable debuffs now show on
  nameplates, unit frames, and the party and raid frame.
- The auction house scans itself: while it is open, every auction
  is read and kept in your saved files, every 16 minutes, so a
  full price history grows from day one. A bar under the window
  shows the scan start, how far it is and when the next one comes.
  Press the bar to start a scan yourself.
- Gathering is counted: every herb, vein and skinned creature, how
  often you gathered it and what it gave. Rare drops show on the
  tooltip of the node and of the item, with how often they came.
  /sane data gathered lists it all.
- The map's pins show drop rates: hover a rare, a chest, a herb or
  a vein to see what it gives and how often. A dungeon's enemies
  and its bosses' loot show them too.
- Discoveries: Sane UI keeps a list of what the game showed that
  its data lacked, such as a new quest or a drop nobody listed.
  /sane data found shows it, and General has two chat messages
  for new data, both off at first.
- Drop counts and discoveries are shared too, with your guild and
  group, every 15 minutes, your own other accounts included.
- Auction prices are shared by themselves: a full scan reaches
  every Sane UI player in your guild and group, your own other
  accounts too. General, Share data with my guild and group. Odd
  prices wait for a second player, and Forget shared data takes
  out all that others sent.
- Tooltips use what the auction house lists right now and your
  last scan for what an item brings, what its materials bring
  disenchanted and what to do with it. With Disenchant, the advice
  weighs that too. The auction history has a file of its own.
- Hold Alt over an item: its Gold section shows how the price went
  at the auction house, over 24 hours, a week, two weeks, a month or
  everything seen so far. Tap Ctrl for the next range. It reads your
  scans and your other accounts', whichever are the most current.
- Distances are in meters and the time in 24 hours everywhere Sane
  UI writes them. Yards and 12 hours: General, Units, Imperial.
- A new level is told in the middle of the screen: your health,
  mana, stats and talent points it brought and how long you played
  on the level before, in place of the game's lines in the chat.
- Stats are written short wherever Sane UI shows them: Str, Stam,
  Agi, Int, Spi, Arm, Crit, Crit dmg, AP, SP and the rest.
- Your alts: the list of characters is a dropdown, which leaves
  room for the items, and the window now works like your bags:
  collapsing groups, categories side by side, Sort By, Grid, List
  and Compact, and the same settings.
- Bags and alts: a slider for the size of the items in the View
  dropdown. In a narrow window they shrink by themselves, and both
  windows can now be made much narrower.
- Bags: the bags you wear always show their icon, and an empty
  slot shows what goes there: a bag, a reagent bag or keys. The
  Armory and Consumables start collapsed.
- Chat: a quiet start. The game's own chat stays out of sight, and each
  character starts in none of the public channels: join one when you
  want it, with /join or in the list on the chat's name. Loot, money,
  experience, General, Trade, Local Defense and Services are not shown
  until you tick them.
- Speed Leveling: the guide knows by itself which steps you have
  done. Quests, objectives and levels count wherever a step
  stands, it no longer waits at a hearth or a vendor you are long
  past, and going back to look at a step keeps your place.
- Unit frames: casts and timers are part of the frames. Your
  cast, your target's and your focus's show directly above the
  health bar. Your breath lies over your health bar in the game's
  bubbles, half see-through; fatigue and feign death stand above
  it. Your pet shows its health and its resource under your frame.
  All in place of the game's own bars.
- Navigation: the travel overlay sits a little higher and holds the
  button to leave a flight or a vehicle, in place of the game's.
- Bags: Auto Sell and Auto Repair, two checkboxes at the foot of
  your bags, on at first. At a vendor your junk is sold, then your
  gear repaired with your own gold. Hold Shift to do it yourself.
- Profiles: every character uses one of three, or one you make.
  Speedleveling has all the automation on and is where new
  characters start for now; Accountwide has our recommended
  settings, shared by all its characters; Character specific is
  the game's defaults, as a fresh install, each character its own.
  A profile holds everything: key bindings, Edit Mode layouts, all
  game settings, where windows stand, the addons switched on.
  Accountwide and Speedleveling set our recommended view distance,
  grass and shadows, 115 frames a second, quicker loading screens
  and the camera's farthest zoom for you; what you change afterwards
  stays yours.
- Speed Leveling: nothing holds you up. Loot takes everything by
  default, the game no longer asks before equipping or destroying,
  the key runs you to the corpses around you and opens them, and
  once a quest is taken or handed in it leaves the NPC alone and
  closes the goodbye screen. Quest rewards are chosen for you: the
  biggest upgrade, else what is worth the most, and upgrades you
  loot are put on. On group rolls you Need your upgrades and Greed
  the rest. Cinematics are skipped. Its key is E unless you pick
  another. Each has its switch, and a
  box Speed Leveling in the bags or in the minimap's satchel turns the profile on or off. A right click unlocks the guide and the
  arrow to drag them.
- Speed Leveling: before the guide goes on to a harder zone, a step
  of its own for each profession that is too low for it.
- Bags: Make Room keeps one slot free. When the last one fills,
  your cheapest junk goes at your next key press; with no junk you
  get a warning on the screen.
- Bags: a green arrow on the items that would be an upgrade, by
  what your class and specialization want.
- Combat: your frame, your target's and your focus's in the look
  of the nameplates, green: level and raid marker on the left,
  then the name (players by their full name; none on your own),
  the health and how much is left on the right. Under your
  target's and focus's frames their target, under yours your
  pet, as a cast bar under a nameplate. Under the health the
  power, half as high, with "current | percent" (a druid's mana
  and its form's power); around them the three most important
  buffs, debuffs and crowd control, with your own marks (click an
  icon, or /sane auras). Placed in
  Edit Mode. The action bars are clean: no borders or
  decorations, only the buttons that hold something.
- Lore: talking to an NPC is a scene over the world. The NPC's
  face, name and words stand see-through low in the middle, a
  paragraph at a time, your answers beside them, the quest's
  rewards under them, while the rest of the interface fades out
  (tooltips stay, to compare rewards). Each reward says what it
  is (cloth, leather, mail, plate, a weapon), whether you can use
  it, what it sells for and what it changes against what you
  wear. Space goes on.
- Loot: a loot window of Sane UI's own at your mouse: each
  thing a bar like the nameplates', in its quality's colour (a
  quest's in gold), with what a vendor pays for it and an A to
  take it at once from now on; Link and Close at the foot.
  A green arrow marks gear that would make your character
  better, and such upgrades always wait in the window to be
  seen (Always show upgrades). Click to take and the next
  comes under the mouse. Nothing is
  taken at once until you say so: everything, or coins,
  currencies, quest items, trade goods, gear, consumables or
  junk, as soon as the loot is ready; the options list your
  items, ticked where they are taken at once. Master looters
  give things from a menu. Bags full? A page beside it shows
  what the thing is worth and the cheapest thing you could drop.
  Group rolls come as cards that show who chose what, and
  what you and your group receive rises over the world.
- Speed Leveling: your leveling guide over the world, and one key
  for questing - on its own, with no other addon needed. No
  window, just clear words on the game: the zone, how far you
  are, the steps to do now with their counts, and the next steps
  fading out. Click a step to skip it, right-click to go back.
  Right-click it to choose a guide: the guides that come for
  each game are there, by group. In WoW: Forever it warns when
  your Herbalism or Mining is too low for the next zone the
  guide takes you to, so you gather a while first. Open the Guides window
  (/sane guides) to paste a guide you bought - it opens for your
  own account - or any guide's text; Sane UI runs it by itself as
  you quest. Pause it or switch it off in the minimap's satchel. Over it, the items to
  use and the NPCs and creatures to target, as icons you click. A
  leather arrow of its own, placed in Edit Mode, points the way:
  in it a picture of what to do there (talk, fight, loot, use),
  under it what to do, how far and how long it takes.
- Speed Leveling's key: one button loots the corpse by you, talks
  to the quest giver in front of you and takes its quests, picks
  up quest things, attacks your foe, or finds and marks the next
  target - whatever makes sense where you stand. It runs you to
  the NPC it found, and uses your quest item on the corpse or the
  creature your guide says it is for.
- The minimap's satchel: one button on the minimap opens a page
  with the zone you are in, your time and the realm's, frame
  rate, latency, new mail, the game's calendar, clock (with its
  alarm) and stopwatch; the buttons other addons put round the
  minimap (and the game's addon menu), round; Sane UI's own
  switches, such as your leveling guide on, paused or off; every
  kind of pin and what the game's minimap tracks, in large cells
  you switch with a click.
- Navigation: a middle click on the map brings it back to you,
  following you again, near enough to see the minimap's icons.
- Speed Leveling: the map pin goes on each place your guide sends
  you to, so the golden line on the map leads you there.
- Quest log: sorted by proximity, nearest first; click its title
  to sort by level, zone or type, click beside it to hide the list.
  A click on a quest unfolds its story, objectives and rewards under
  it, a double click puts the map pin on it, so the golden line leads
  you there. An open quest reads like a page: headings in gold, its
  gold and experience, its rewards as big icons, the mouse on one for
  its tooltip. A click on an exclamation mark on the map adds its
  quests on top, a red ! and a red X saying they are not in your log:
  that X or a right click takes them out. A + on a quest's level says
  it is meant for a group, and objectives start with how many and a
  sword or a sack: 4/7, sack, Nightstalker. Quest
  items stand beside their quests, on the right half of the screen
  it mirrors.
  The button Quest Log on the full map turns it on or off. On, it
  also takes the place of the game's quest tracker on the normal
  screen: in its own spot (top left at first), clicked through,
  showing only what is still to do.
- Retail: Sane UI works in the main game too, beside WoW: Forever.
  Everything that both games have works in both; what only Forever
  has (its painted Atlas, its data of the world, Combat's own
  spellbook and talents, the trainers' spells in the spellbook)
  stays in Forever for now, and the game's own takes its place.
  The game's settings Sane UI keeps for you stay with the game
  they were made in.
- Navigation in Retail: the map shows the game's own maps, one
  at a time, as the game's map does: click a zone to go in,
  right-click to go up to the continent, Azeroth and every
  world, scroll to zoom. WoW: Forever keeps its own map of the
  whole world.
- Speed Leveling: one key for questing. Give it a key (E, say)
  under Sane UI in the game's key bindings and press it again and
  again: it opens the loot of the corpse beside you, picks up the
  quest's plant or chest you stand at, finds what your quests and
  your leveling guide want around you, puts a skull on it, and
  attacks it or talks to it. When your guide says to hearth, it
  hearths; when nothing is around, it uses your quest item.
- Navigation: far herbs and ore. Every herb and ore your minimap
  finds now shows on the screen however far away it is: the picture
  of what it gives in a ring of stitched leather that points the way
  to it, and how many yards away it is under it. One behind you or
  outside your view waits at the edge of the screen, its point
  showing which way to turn. On the minimap the found herbs and ore
  wear the same leather ring, round.
- Data: Sane UI's database gets better as you play. Every quest you
  are offered, take, do and hand in is checked against it: its
  giver, objectives, rewards and who takes it back are confirmed, and
  a quest Sane UI did not know yet is learned as you do it. Loot is
  counted, so the chances of drops become real numbers over time;
  the places of quest givers, vendors, herbs and ore are narrowed to
  where you stood; creatures, vendors' goods and spells are checked
  too. What your play teaches is kept in your saved files, for the
  next versions of Sane UI to take in.
- Inventory: the Character Stats window is redrawn on the journal's
  page. Your stats are no longer one long narrow list: each category
  is a section written in pencil, and the sections stand side by side
  in as many columns as the window is wide; narrow, it is one list.
  With room enough, your five attributes stand on top in boxes with
  their numbers written large; your pet's stats follow yours. The gear
  sets and the titles are windows of their own now, drawn in pencil
  too.
- Inventory: every row of the bags' sidebar has a small pencil drawing
  of its own instead of the game's tiny icons: a backpack for All
  Items, a helmet for Head, a flask for Potions, a campfire for Camp
  Items. A new category, Containers, holds the bags and quivers you
  carry and what you open with a right click (lockboxes, satchels,
  clams). The same drawing, in grey, marks each category's empty
  slot in your bags, and all words in the bags, the bank and the alts
  are larger. A group's categories (The
  Armory, Adventure Prep) are written darker, so they are easier to
  read.
- Combat text of Sane UI's own: damage, critical hits and heals rise
  over the nameplate of the creature or player that took them, and
  over your own character, in Sane UI's handwriting. A miss, dodge,
  parry, block, evade or immunity shows as a pencil drawing instead of
  a word. It takes the place of the game's own combat text.
- Nameplates: everything on one line inside the bar, each part only
  when there is something to show, and the bar just as wide as the
  line: your quest objective on the creature (a sword or a sack and
  "0/7"), its raid marker, its level, a player's item level ("i32"),
  your threat (grey, orange when you are about to pull, red with a red
  glow around the plate when you have aggro), the name, and health
  with its percent ("1.32m | 100%"). The unit's mana,
  rage or energy as a thin line along the bottom, your combo points on
  it. A gold border on bosses and elites, silver on rares. Your
  debuffs right on top of the bar, a foe's buffs left, crowd control
  right. Plates of players you do not know are hidden, and plates are
  smaller the farther away they are.
- Navigation: a click on the map pings the place to your party or
  raid, as a click on the game's minimap does; a double click puts
  the map pin there.
  A click on a quest in the quest log moves the map to it. The box
  Follow me on the full map's bar keeps you in the map's middle.
- Data you can trust: what Sane UI's data has not had confirmed by
  the game says "Unconfirmed Data" until you or somebody of your
  guild or group sees it in play: an NPC at its place, a mailbox
  opened, a boss's loot looted after the kill, its abilities cast.

0.2.0 (2026-10-14)
------------------
- Modules you can switch on and off. In the game's addon list, Sane UI now has
  its modules under it: Settings and Spellbook. Untick the ones
  you do not want. What a module saved is kept until you switch it on again.

- Options with a page per module. Esc > Options > AddOns > Sane UI is the
  general page; each module that is switched on has its own page under it.

- If you update from 0.1.0: copy all three folders (SaneUI, SaneUI_Settings,
  SaneUI_Spellbook) into your AddOns folder and restart the game.


0.1.0 (2026-10-14)
------------------
The first version of Sane UI.

- Spells you have not learned yet, in your spellbook. They are listed in grey,
  in the tab where they will end up, with the level or trainer they come from.

- Reserve action bar buttons. Drag a grey spell onto a button: the button waits
  for it and becomes the real spell the moment you learn it.

- Training: a tab in the spellbook shows what your trainer has for you, and
  what it costs.

- Action bar layouts. Save all your bars under a name and load them again later.

- Options in the game's own menu: Esc > Options > AddOns > Sane UI, or type /sane.
